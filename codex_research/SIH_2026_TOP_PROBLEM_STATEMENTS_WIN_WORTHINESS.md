## Source and scoring note

The current SIH detail pages were not consistently discoverable from the public official website during this research pass. Problem wording was cross-checked through recent community archives that report a 21 August 2026 snapshot of `sih.gov.in`, while the judging framework comes from the official SIH guidelines. The official guidance says evaluation considers novelty, complexity, clarity, feasibility, practicability, sustainability, scale of impact, user experience, and future-work potential. Before committing, the team must verify the statement, attached dataset, submission count, and deadline inside the official SIH portal. The widely mirrored deadline is 20 September 2026, but a mirror is not the final authority. Sources used include the [official SIH guidelines](https://www.sih.gov.in/letters/Guidelines-College-SPOC.pdf) and the [SIH 2026 archive snapshot](https://sih2026.vuce.in/en).

The supplied F/G/M/S/W totals are treated as the team's prior ranking because the meanings of those initials were not provided. This report independently evaluates “win-worthiness”: not social importance alone, but the probability that a six-person student team can submit a distinctive idea, produce a real and reliable prototype, validate its central claim, deliver an excellent live demonstration, and survive expert questioning. A high score does not mean the problem is easy. It means its difficulty can be converted into visible, defensible competitive advantage.

## Executive conclusion

**SIH26158, Drone-to-3D, remains the strongest choice for this team at 8.8/10 win-worthiness.** It has the best combination of technical depth, visual demonstration, strategic importance, measurable outputs, and a distinctive trust/provenance story. More importantly, substantial focused research has already reduced its execution risk. It only remains number one if the team immediately proves a real reconstruction on the available hardware; without that proof, the project’s apparent advantage can disappear quickly.

**SIH26143, oil-spill detection with AIS attribution, is the strongest alternative at 8.4/10.** Its detect–hindcast–attribute narrative is unusually clear and memorable, and much of the data ecosystem is public. It is arguably easier to communicate than Drone-to-3D, but SAR look-alikes and uncertain vessel attribution create serious scientific and legal credibility risks.

**SIH26037, adaptive path planning for Indian roads, is the best controlled-simulation alternative at 8.1/10.** MathWorks specifies scenarios, tools, outputs, and metrics clearly. It is highly demonstrable and testable, though likely to attract strong competition and requires access to the prescribed MathWorks ecosystem.

The next tier contains the MALE UAV engine digital twin and Antarctic navigation problems. Both are compelling but data- and domain-heavy. The lower-ranked statements are not unimportant; they are harder to validate honestly within the available time because they depend on unavailable municipal drainage networks, rare flash-flood labels, sensitive investigation data, scarce sonar debris imagery, clinical validation, or expensive paired satellite imagery.

## 1. SIH26158 — Single-Pass Drone Video to Accurate 3D Model Generation System

The NTRO statement asks for an AI-enabled system that converts a single moving-UAV video, GPS coordinates, and flight metadata into a georeferenced, metrically accurate representation of visible terrain, structures, facades, rooftops, roads, infrastructure, vegetation, and obstacles. Outputs may include point clouds or textured meshes and must support visualization, measurement, and analysis. The statement explicitly identifies limited viewing angles, blur and compression, illumination variation, dynamic objects, GPS error, near-real-time processing, occlusion, and metric accuracy without extensive GCPs as central challenges. The mandatory inputs are 1080p/4K video, GPS, and flight metadata; IMU, barometric altitude, camera intrinsics, and RTK/PPK are optional. The mirrored full statement is available at [SIH26158](https://sih2026.vuce.in/en/ps/SIH26158).

This is exceptionally win-worthy because the result is visually immediate yet technically deep: a judge can watch real footage become a navigable 3D model and then test a distance or height. The strongest differentiation is not merely calling COLMAP or a neural model. It is Drishti3D’s evidence-aware architecture: observed high-confidence, observed low-confidence, AI-assisted, dynamic-excluded, and unobserved geometry remain separate, and inferred surfaces are excluded from measurement by default. That directly resolves the statement’s most scientifically dangerous requirement—occluded-surface reconstruction—without fabricating certainty.

The risk is considerable. Monocular video has scale ambiguity; a single trajectory may provide weak baseline and few loop closures; ordinary GNSS limits absolute accuracy; rolling shutter and stabilization can damage geometry; dense reconstruction and MASt3R/VGGT stress an 8 GB GPU. A polished viewer with a prebuilt model will not survive expert scrutiny. The win gate is therefore concrete: before final commitment, a real 30–60 second team video must produce registered camera poses and a genuine point cloud, GNSS alignment must recover a plausible metric scale, and a known distance must be measured within an honestly declared tolerance. If that gate passes, this remains the best pick. **Win-worthiness: 8.8/10. Recommendation: primary choice.**

## 2. SIH26059 — AI-Enabled Antarctic Sea-Ice, Iceberg Trajectory, and Navigation Decision Support

The Ministry of Earth Sciences/NCPOR statement asks for a decision-support platform that forecasts Antarctic sea-ice concentration, predicts iceberg trajectories, and recommends safe, fuel-efficient research-vessel routes using satellite, oceanographic, and meteorological data. The public mirror provides only a concise description, so the team must inspect the attached resources and evaluation expectations on the official portal before relying on any inferred deliverables. See [SIH26059](https://sih2026.vuce.in/en/ps/SIH26059).

This problem has a powerful mission story and an excellent map-based demonstration: animate evolving ice concentration and uncertain iceberg tracks, then show a route changing to minimize collision risk, transit time, and fuel use. Public sources such as sea-ice concentration products, satellite imagery, ERA5 winds, and ocean-current reanalysis make an MVP plausible. A strong solution would use probabilistic forecasts, ensemble trajectory uncertainty, a time-dependent risk field, and multi-objective routing rather than drawing a static shortest path.

Its weakness is scientific breadth. Sea-ice forecasting, iceberg drift, ocean currents, uncertainty propagation, ship–ice constraints, and fuel-aware routing are separate research problems. Antarctic observations are sparse, and “safe” depends on vessel ice class and operational rules. A team can make an impressive visual product while lacking credible predictive skill. It becomes competitive only if narrowed to a specific region and historical voyage, with hindcast validation and calibrated uncertainty. **Win-worthiness: 7.9/10. Recommendation: strong alternative for a team already skilled in geospatial forecasting and ocean data.**

## 3. SIH26143 — Satellite Oil-Spill Detection with AIS Vessel Attribution

The NTRO statement asks teams to use satellite imagery to detect oil spills at sea and correlate the result with Automatic Identification System vessel tracks to identify the vessel potentially responsible. The strongest implementation is a three-stage evidentiary pipeline: segment and characterize a possible slick in SAR imagery, use winds and currents to hindcast a probabilistic origin region and time window, then reconstruct AIS traffic and rank vessels using proximity, course alignment, timing, and anomalies such as suspicious AIS gaps. The listing is mirrored in the [Space Technology statements](https://sih2026.vuce.in/en/themes/space-technology), and an end-to-end oil-spill/AIS association has precedent in peer-reviewed work such as this [SAR/AIS attribution study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0964569121002660).

This is one of the most compelling SIH narratives: detect environmental harm, reconstruct where it came from, and present an evidence-backed suspect ranking on a map. Sentinel-1 SAR, wind/current reanalysis, oil-spill datasets, AIS samples, and mature Lagrangian drift tooling make the architecture achievable. The demo has a natural beginning, middle, and end, and each subsystem can expose meaningful uncertainty.

The principal risk is false accusation. Low-wind zones, biogenic films, ship wakes, and other SAR look-alikes can resemble mineral oil. Hindcasting is uncertain and errors compound before AIS scoring. The output must be a ranked hypothesis with a probability region and evidence trail, never “this vessel is guilty.” Validation should measure slick precision/recall, hindcast containment, and whether the known source vessel ranks near the top on historical cases. **Win-worthiness: 8.4/10. Recommendation: best fallback if Drone-to-3D fails its early technical gate.**

## 4. SIH26054 — AI-Enabled Digital Twin for MALE UAV Aero-Piston Engine Health

The DRDO statement asks for a modular, continuously synchronized virtual representation of a MALE UAV aero-piston engine. It combines sensor ingestion, thermodynamic behavior, performance maps, degradation logic, AI/ML analytics, anomaly detection, remaining-useful-life estimation, mission replay, environmental simulation, operator dashboards, and future CAN/SocketCAN or ECU/FADEC integration. Required monitoring includes RPM, CHT, EGT, oil pressure/temperature, fuel flow, vibration, electrical health, and injection timing. Fault targets include misfire, injector anomalies, cooling/lubrication degradation, sensor failure, combustion instability, overheating, and abnormal vibration. Full requirements are mirrored at [SIH26054](https://sih2026.vuce.in/en/ps/SIH26054).

The problem scores well because DRDO permits demonstration with simulated or real engine datasets, creating room for a physics-guided synthetic engine model. A strong prototype would not be a dashboard with random telemetry. It would connect a simplified thermodynamic/state model to injected faults, train anomaly and RUL components on controlled degradation runs, show explanation and uncertainty, and replay a mission in which maintenance advice changes before a threshold alarm fires. Edge deployment and secure telemetry strengthen the future-deployment story.

The central weakness is the absence of representative aero-piston degradation data and OEM performance maps. Without engine-domain expertise, a synthetic model can become circular: the team injects a pattern and the ML detects the same pattern. RUL cannot be credibly validated without run-to-failure or degradation evidence. Judges from propulsion/reliability backgrounds will identify a decorative “digital twin” quickly. **Win-worthiness: 7.8/10. Recommendation: choose only with a mechanical/aerospace mentor and a defensible engine simulation/data plan.**

## 5. SIH26037 — Adaptive Path Planning and Collision Avoidance on Unstructured Indian Roads

The MathWorks statement asks for a closed-loop autonomous-driving simulation covering perception, short-term agent prediction, decision logic, real-time path planning, and vehicle motion in unstructured Indian conditions. The system must handle mixed road users, unclear or missing lanes, informal merging, unpredictable pedestrians, potholes/road-edge ambiguity, and unexpected obstacles. It requires at least five realistic scenarios, including an unmarked village road, unsignalized busy intersection, slow-vehicle highway merge, dense market, and sudden cattle crossing, with at least two detailed RoadRunner scenes. Deliverables include MATLAB/Simulink models, scenarios, metrics such as replanning latency, path smoothness and completion rate, and a demonstration video. The full listing is at [SIH26037](https://sih2026.vuce.in/en/ps/SIH26037).

This is highly winnable because the sponsor defines the environment, expected tools, scenarios, and metrics. Simulation makes repeated quantitative testing possible, and the live visual is intuitive. A strong team can differentiate with uncertainty-aware prediction for non-lane-based agents, risk-sensitive planning, interpretable Stateflow decisions, adversarial scenario generation, and a safety case showing collisions, near misses, jerk, path efficiency, and replanning latency over many randomized runs.

The disadvantages are tool dependence, likely high participation, and integration breadth. A team that merely runs a supplied planner through attractive RoadRunner scenes will look generic. Licensing/toolbox availability and learning time must be confirmed immediately. The winning artifact is a reproducible scenario benchmark plus a planner that clearly beats baselines such as static A*, deterministic MPC, or rule-only logic. **Win-worthiness: 8.1/10. Recommendation: strongest controlled-simulation option.**

## 6. SIH26161 — Dam-Break Inundation Modelling for Any River

The NTRO problem asks for an automated framework that models dam failure, sudden release, or natural river blockage failure using hydrological data, DEMs, and satellite imagery. It specifically asks for Smooth Particle Hydrodynamics and Delft3D scenario comparison, inundation and lower-catchment impact analysis, a GUI, support for large datasets, SHP/KML output, near-real-time flood analysis with open data/Google Earth Engine, and a final demonstration on an Indian river and dam. The detailed mirrored statement is at [SIH26161](https://sih-fit.vercel.app/problem/SIH26161).

Few demonstrations are more dramatic than a flood wave moving down a real valley, with depth, arrival time, affected settlements, and evacuation consequences appearing on a map. Open DEMs and satellite flood extents are available, and a team can build around validated solvers rather than inventing CFD. Automation of terrain preprocessing, breach scenario definition, solver orchestration, comparison, and GIS export is a legitimate systems contribution.

The difficulty is validation and computational physics. Breach hydrograph, boundary conditions, DEM artifacts, numerical stability, and roughness can dominate the result. Catchment-scale SPH may be impractical, and Delft3D expertise is not trivial. A plausible animation is easy; proving it is correct is hard. This problem is worth selecting only with hydrology/CFD mentorship and a historical case with observed inundation for validation. **Win-worthiness: 7.3/10. Recommendation: high-impact specialist choice, not a general software-team choice.**

## 7. SIH26189 — AI-Powered Criminal Network Analysis System

The Ministry of Home Affairs statement asks for a system that ingests structured and unstructured investigative data—reports/FIR-like text, call records, financial transactions, surveillance notes and identifiers—extracts people, phones, vehicles, locations and organizations, builds a relationship graph, identifies influential actors and communities, detects suspicious patterns, and gives investigators an interactive link-analysis workspace. The title and theme are confirmed in the [SIH 2026 problem list](https://github.com/jeevansai-hub/SIH-2026-/blob/main/ps_2026/README.md), with a detailed mirrored description at [SIH26189](https://sih-buddy.vercel.app/ps/SIH26189).

This is feasible and demo-friendly. An investigator can upload documents and records, resolve entities across spellings and aliases, explore a graph, inspect why an edge exists, and see a central actor or suspicious subnetwork emerge. Graph algorithms, NLP, Neo4j/NetworkX, and Cytoscape-like visualization are mature. A strong entry would make every entity merge and risk score evidence-linked, reversible, and human-reviewable.

The problem’s weakness is data authenticity and novelty. Real investigation data is sensitive, so teams will use synthetic records whose hidden network they already constructed. Commercial link-analysis systems exist. Clean synthetic text avoids the true bottleneck: multilingual, misspelled, partial, contradictory entity resolution. False links can materially harm people, so explainability and role-based access are mandatory. It can win only if the team creates a genuinely difficult, auditable benchmark and proves entity-resolution quality rather than displaying an impressive graph. **Win-worthiness: 7.0/10. Recommendation: feasible fallback, but weaker novelty and evidence than the top choices.**

## 8. SIH26057 — Marine Debris and Anomaly Detection from Side-Scan Sonar

The Ministry of Earth Sciences statement asks for AI-powered detection of underwater debris and man-made anomalies in side-scan sonar imagery. The expected class set reportedly includes objects such as wrecks, pipes, cylinders, and entangled netting, with robustness to speckle, shadows, changing pixel resolution, vehicle motion/dropout, and natural seabed clutter. A useful solution must correct sonar geometry, detect or segment targets, suppress false positives, attach confidence, recover location/size from ping metadata, and give operators an annotated waterfall plus geotagged report. The title is confirmed by the [MoES theme listing](https://sih2026.vuce.in/en/themes/renewable-sustainable-energy), with expanded mirrored details at [SIH26057](https://sih-buddy.vercel.app/ps/SIH26057).

This is visually distinctive and likely less crowded than ordinary vision problems. Detection overlays on acoustic imagery, followed by coordinates and an exportable recovery survey, make a coherent demo. Edge-optimized inference for an AUV and calibrated false-positive filtering offer meaningful differentiation.

The decisive obstacle is data. Public side-scan collections are small and often emphasize wrecks or aircraft rather than ghost nets and diverse debris. Raw sonar is not a photograph: slant-range distortion, nadir gaps, variable along-track spacing, speckle, and acoustic shadows require domain-specific preprocessing. Training a detector on unrelated objects and calling it debris detection will not survive a specialist judge. **Win-worthiness: 6.9/10. Recommendation: choose only if the official attached dataset contains adequate labelled targets or the team has a marine-acoustics partner.**

## 9. SIH26085 — Urban Flood Nowcasting with Drainage–Rainfall Coupling

The Ministry of Earth Sciences statement asks for short-horizon urban flood prediction that couples rainfall nowcasts and high-resolution surface runoff with a stormwater drainage network. The intended system models inlets, manholes, pipes/canals and their capacities, determines surcharge and surface backup, produces street-level inundation depth over roughly the next few hours, and can support flood-safe routing. The title is confirmed in the [SIH disaster-management listing](https://sih2026.vuce.in/hi/themes/disaster-management), with detailed mirrored interpretation at [SIH26085](https://sih-buddy.vercel.app/ps/SIH26085).

The product story is excellent: replay a known cloudburst, show drains reaching capacity, predict flooded junctions, and reroute an ambulance. Coupling surface and network processes is more meaningful than simply coloring a rainfall map. Historical event replay provides a path to validation if observed waterlogging locations are available.

Unfortunately, the statement depends on data rarely available publicly at useful resolution: actual municipal drain topology, invert levels, pipe dimensions, capacity and condition, plus metre-scale terrain that resolves streets and flow paths. A synthetic drainage network can demonstrate software mechanics but cannot support authoritative street-depth claims. This makes the problem far less winnable unless a municipality or provided dataset supplies a real ward network. **Win-worthiness: 6.3/10. Recommendation: do not choose without confirming the official dataset solves the drainage-data gap.**

## 10. SIH26192 — Flash-Flood Prediction for Hilly Regions Using Multi-Source Data

The Ministry of Home Affairs statement asks for hyper-local village/ward flash-flood warnings by fusing rainfall, soil moisture, terrain/slope stability, historical landslide and disaster inventories, and real-time IoT sensors. The desired system should provide actionable evacuation lead time and present warnings through a geographic decision-support interface. The statement is summarized by the [SIH problem explorer](https://sih26ps.vercel.app/) and listed in the [SIH 2026 archive](https://github.com/jeevansai-hub/SIH-2026-/blob/main/ps_2026/README.md).

The social impact and demo narrative are very strong. A rigorous prototype could replay a known event, compute antecedent saturation and rainfall intensity-duration thresholds, combine these with terrain susceptibility, and show a probabilistic warning before the reported impact. Physics-informed thresholds plus calibrated ML would be more defensible than an opaque classifier.

The problem asks for a spatial and temporal precision that existing data may not support. Dense real-time soil-moisture sensors are not deployed across most target villages; gauge/radar coverage and historical labels are sparse; flash floods develop rapidly; and landslide susceptibility is related but not identical to flood discharge. A dashboard can easily imply false warning skill. Without a well-documented regional dataset and hindcast validation, expert judges will challenge the claimed lead time. **Win-worthiness: 6.5/10. Recommendation: high-impact but data-constrained; select only with a specific catchment and validated history.**

## 11. SIH26142 — Deep-Learning Super-Resolution Mapping from Satellite Imagery

The NTRO statement asks for a model that converts medium-resolution imagery—specifically 10 m Sentinel-2 input—into a sharper product below 4 m while preserving geographic registration and spectral consistency. Teams may use CNNs, transformers, GANs, diffusion, or other generative approaches, but must train on paired data, assess accuracy against high-resolution references, manage uncertainty, and prove analytical usefulness for tasks such as crop monitoring, road/building visibility, urban analysis, change detection, or disaster assessment. The full mirrored statement is at [SIH26142](https://sih2026.vuce.in/en/ps/SIH26142).

This is tightly scoped and gives an outstanding before–output–truth demonstration. It can be evaluated using image metrics, spectral-angle/error metrics, georegistration, uncertainty, and—most persuasively—whether a downstream segmentation or change-detection task improves. The statement itself recognizes hallucination risk, allowing an honest team to turn fidelity and uncertainty into its main innovation.

The core difficulty is genuine paired training data. High-resolution commercial imagery may be restricted or expensive, public reference coverage may be limited, and co-registration/time differences can make nominal pairs inconsistent. Pixel metrics such as PSNR/SSIM can reward smooth or visually plausible outputs without proving that a narrow road really exists. Since satellite super-resolution is a mature research area, merely fine-tuning SwinIR or Real-ESRGAN is unlikely to win. **Win-worthiness: 7.2/10. Recommendation: good focused ML alternative if paired data and geospatial evaluation are secured immediately.**

## 12. SIH26038 — Explainable AI for Diabetic-Retinopathy Screening in Rural India

The MathWorks statement asks for a MATLAB-based retinal screening pipeline that performs fundus-image quality assessment and recapture guidance, enhancement, optic disc/fovea/vessel and lesion analysis, five-level diabetic-retinopathy grading, lesion-level explainability and calibrated confidence, annotated clinical reporting, and a Simulink model of district-scale telemedicine throughput. It specifies more than 90% sensitivity and more than 85% specificity for referable DR, human review in under 30 seconds, and simulation of a program serving more than 100,000 patients annually. The full mirrored statement is at [SIH26038](https://sih2026.vuce.in/en/ps/SIH26038).

The problem is clear, socially important, supplied with explicit quantitative gates, supported by public retinal datasets, and easy to demonstrate. A strong entry would treat image-quality rejection as a primary safety feature, provide lesion evidence rather than Grad-CAM alone, calibrate probability, evaluate across devices/demographics/datasets, and optimize the complete screening workflow rather than presenting a classifier.

It is nevertheless a crowded domain with a high validation burden. Public benchmarks do not prove rural field performance on portable cameras. Microaneurysm-level labels are limited, domain shift is severe, and a “clinically useful” explanation requires ophthalmologist review. Medical claims must be framed as screening/decision support, not diagnosis. Tool constraints and the breadth of segmentation, grading, XAI, calibration, and Simulink operations make this larger than it first appears. **Win-worthiness: 7.1/10. Recommendation: choose only with ophthalmology access, strong MATLAB experience, and a cross-dataset validation plan.**

## Final selection recommendation

The team should continue with **SIH26158 Drone-to-3D** because it has the highest ceiling and the greatest amount of already completed strategic research. It uniquely combines a memorable demonstration with deep computer vision, geospatial engineering, measurable accuracy, offline operational relevance, and a novel trust model. The existing RTX 4060 system is sufficient for a carefully scoped classical sparse/medium-density path, with learned reconstruction treated as optional.

That recommendation is conditional. Within the next technical gate, the team must obtain one real drone clip and synchronized telemetry, install or containerize FFmpeg and COLMAP, produce a genuine reconstruction, align it to ENU, and measure a known dimension. If this cannot be made repeatable, pivot early—not after building the UI.

The recommended fallback order is **SIH26143 oil-spill/AIS attribution**, then **SIH26037 Indian-road adaptive planning**, then **SIH26059 Antarctic navigation**, with **SIH26054 engine digital twin** only if engine-domain mentorship and data become available. The remaining problems should be selected only when their specific missing-data constraint is resolved by the official attached dataset or an external partner.

The choice should not be made from the numerical ranking alone. The winning problem is the highest-ranked one for which this exact team can produce exclusive evidence. A real drone and calibration knowledge favor SIH26158; SAR/ocean expertise favors SIH26143; MATLAB/RoadRunner mastery favors SIH26037; ocean forecasting favors SIH26059. Team capability and data access are multipliers, not minor adjustments.
